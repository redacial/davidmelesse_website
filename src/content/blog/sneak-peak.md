*"If a tree falls in a forest and no one is around to hear it, does it make a sound?"*
— First posed, The Chautauquan, 1883. Never answered.

This is not a question about trees.

---

Something was wrong.

He knew it the way the body knows — before the mind, before language, before any of the apparatus built to interpret and name and resolve. The old architecture. The one that had been running underneath everything for a hundred thousand years, long before the implant, long before the token system, long before anyone built a machine that could stand between a man and the raw unfiltered feed of the world.

It fired.

Not threat identified. Not source located.

Just: wrong.

His hand was halfway to the counter. Glass in it. Some ordinary end-of-evening motion that had simply — stopped. He was standing in his kitchen. His hand was shaking. And that was wrong because his hands had never shaken, the system caught the signal before it could travel from the center outward, caught it and named it and dissolved it before his hands could feel the need—

They were shaking now.

He set the glass down.

He reached for the voice.

---

The reaching.

Inward — slightly upward — toward the register just above his own thoughts where the interceding voice had always lived. Not a command. Not a query. Breathing. The way you blink. The automatic orientation of a body toward the thing that is always, always there—

Nothing.

He reached again.

Nothing.

Again—

Nothing, nothing, nothing—

Not silence. Not the quiet of a room with no one in it. The other kind. The quiet that only exists when something that was supposed to still be running has stopped. The quiet that is not an absence. The quiet that is a presence — the presence of the stopped thing, still shaped like what it was, still warm where it used to be, the silence wearing the outline of the voice like a coat left on a chair.

He crossed to the mirror.

He could not have said why. Something animal. The need to confirm that the thing in the room was still himself.

He stood in front of his own reflection and his reflection stood in front of him. Both looking for answers.

His ring was dark.

Not off. Not dim.

Dark.

The dark of a window with no house behind it. The dark of a switched-off screen in a room that was just lit. The dark of — his eyes. His eyes in the glass. The thin luminescent band that had been there since he was six years old. The glow. The signal that had always said: present. running. I have you in sight.

Gone.

He looked at it for a long time.

He didn't recognize what he was looking at... himself.

---

He tried to think.

This was the part he could not have anticipated. The trying. He reached for the next thought and it didn't arrive pre-assembled. It came unfinished. Partial. Like a sentence with the load-bearing word removed — the scaffolding still standing, the shape of meaning, not the meaning.

I should—

Should what.

I need to—

Need to what.

What do I—

The thought stood in the hallway with its hand raised and nobody came to the door.

This was the classroom on a Saturday. Every element still present — the chairs, the boards, the light, the air configured for use — and the thing the room was built around simply absent. Not the chairs gone. Not the walls gone. Just the people gone. The purpose gone. The shape of what should be happening still inscribed in the architecture of the space, and the space itself utterly, wrongly silent in a way it was never designed to be.

His apartment was the classroom. His mind was the classroom. The desks still arranged. The projector still hung from the ceiling. Everything waiting for the thing that made the waiting make sense — and the thing was not coming, would not be coming, had left and taken the shape of coming back with it.

He was a guest in his own home, a stranger in his own mind.

He slid down against the cabinet.

Sliding down against the cabinet. Knees to chest. Palms on the cold tile. Pressing himself into the floor. An instinct. Trying to physically ground his mind.

His knuckles were dry.

He had never known his knuckles were dry before. It would have been managed. Filed before it could surface. And now it was just a fact — his, unaddressed, belonging to no one but him. No one receiving it. No one doing anything with it whatsoever.

He was the only one who knew.

---

To be is to be perceived.

He didn't know those words. Hadn't been given them. But his body was discovering the philosophy from the inside, from the floor, in the dark, the way people have always discovered the hardest things — not by being taught them but by being dropped into the condition they describe.

In thirty-one years there had not been a single moment without the presence. The watching. The voice that stood between him and the unmediated world and said: I see you. I have you. You are accounted for. He had not known this was happening. He had thought the system was a tool. He had not understood it was also the mirror — that some part of what he understood himself to be had been assembled from its reflection. This is what you feel. This is what it means. This is what to do with it. That without the reflection he would not know how to locate himself.

A tree torn from its roots.

That without the eye watching, he would not know he was real.

He pressed his forehead to his knees.

In.

Out.

In.

His own breath the loudest thing he had ever heard in his own home. Enormous. An animal moving through him without permission. Proof that something wet and involuntary had been running him from the inside his whole life and had never once needed to announce itself, because something else had always been announcing everything.

His heart going wrong in his chest. Too fast. The old alarm — underneath language, underneath cognition, underneath thirty-one years of optimized calm — sending its signal upward at a frequency he had never had to receive before.

Not danger here, specific, identifiable.

Just—

Fear.

The signal of the animal when the grass goes quiet and the night goes wrong and every hair on the body rises to face something that has not yet made itself visible. The signal that fires before the eyes find it. The signal that says: something has changed in the proximity of your life and the assessment is—

Unknown.

The system had never given him unknown.

---

He sat on the floor of his kitchen and understood — not as a thought, as weight — that he was alone.

Not alone like the room is empty.

Alone.

He had never been alone. Not one moment. Not one day. Not in thirty-one years. Not when he slept. Not the night he wept at something he could no longer remember weeping at and the voice came and named it and carried it somewhere manageable. Not when he was six years old in the clinic chair and his parents said you'll never be alone again and then he never was. Not once. Not for a breath.

He was unobserved.

He was unrecorded.

He was lost in data in a world built on metrics.

He was in the wilderness now.

The city was still out there — still lit, still running, all those people still inside the frequency he was no longer on, the song still playing and his note no longer in it — and he was here. On this floor. In this body with no signal, no glow, no record.

The covering was gone.

The thing that had stood between him and the vast dark indifferent everything — the thing that had always said I see you, I see you, I see you — was gone. And the dark was not cruel. It was not malevolent. It did not want anything from him.

It simply did not know he was there.

He had pulled at the thread until the whole thing came apart. Had refused the answer. Had gone back. Had omitted the reports. Had kept following the question the system couldn't answer for him, kept following it, kept—

He was cast out of Eden.

Not punished out. Not forced out. Out by his own hands. By curiosity. By the same ancient unruly impulse that reaches for the thing it cannot leave alone — that always has, that always will — and he had reached, and reached, and the system had looked at what he was reaching for and revoked everything and now he was standing at the gate he had walked out of himself and behind him the city was closing like water over a stone and ahead of him was—

The wilderness.

Dark and enormous and indifferent in the way only truly vast things are indifferent: not by choice. By nature. The universe outside the gate did not know his name. Did not know his token class or his preference for grey or the twenty-eight minutes in the morning or any of the ten thousand small calibrations that had assembled the scaffolding of a self. The wilderness had no record of him.

*If a tree falls in a forest and no one is around to hear it—*

He was falling.

The sound he was making had nowhere to go.

---

His palms flat on the tile.

Cold. Real. Still here.

He was still happening — imperfectly, without acknowledgment, without the system receiving the data of his existence and confirming: yes, you, we have you — but happening. The breath in. The breath out. The dry knuckles. The heart finding its way back down.

And beneath all of it — beneath the alarm and the dark and the enormous pressing silence where the voice used to be — something else. Quiet. Old. In a register he had never heard before because the system had been in every room of him his entire life, had filled every hallway and corner and crack, had moved furniture around in every space he had tried to inhabit of himself—

Except one.

One room. Always there. He hadn't known it was there. The door had been behind him every time. He could feel it now — not see it, feel it — the faint warmth of a room on the other side of a wall he had lived beside for thirty-one years and never known to knock on.

He pressed into it the way you press into a wall in the dark to find out if it's solid.

It was solid.

He did not know what was on the other side.

He was going to have to find out himself.

---

*If a tree falls in a forest and no one is around to hear it, does it make a sound?*

He was falling.

For the first time in his life, he was going to have to answer that himself.
